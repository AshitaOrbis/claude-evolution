# Blog Idea: Tech Influencers and the AGI Meaning Crisis

## Topic
Blog idea about old tech influencers facing a meaning crisis as AGI develops — their followers (and therefore their influence) become structurally irrelevant to the market AI companies are building for. Labs perform courtesy toward them as a salve, but this masks a fundamental irrelevance.

*No URL — concept-only message.*

## Key Findings

- The core insight: tech influencer power derives from the aggregate influence of their followers on purchasing/cultural decisions. As AI targets a market of hundreds of billions of people, the 1-10M tech-literate follower base becomes statistically negligible
- The "bitter entitled" typology: influencers who built identity around being the people who *explain* technology to normals — now being outpaced by technology that explains itself
- Labs are performing courtesy not out of respect but because their own teams are composed of people from the same cultural milieu (tech nerds who grew up respecting these influencers)
- The meaning crisis is sharpest for those whose **self-concept was grounded in being ahead of the curve** — AGI acceleration eliminates the curve entirely, or moves it beyond comprehension
- Adjacent pattern: AI safety communicators, "AI ethicists," and tech journalists face the same structural irrelevance — their audience (educated tech consumers) no longer controls the outcome

## Details

The analytical frame here is **influence as a function of follower leverage over market outcomes**. Traditional tech influencers mattered because:
1. Their followers were early adopters who moved consumer markets
2. Enterprise decision-makers watched consumer tech for signals
3. The tech market was concentrated enough that 1M engaged followers represented a meaningful slice

AGI breaks all three assumptions:
1. The AGI market is ultimately about everyone, not early adopters
2. Enterprise AI decisions are made at the CEO/board level based on strategic positioning, not tech-influencer signals
3. A 10M follower tech channel is statistically invisible in a market targeting global human productivity

The "labs are pretending they matter as a salve" observation is sharp. There's a real social-psychological explanation: the teams building AGI grew up in a world where those tech influencers defined cultural legitimacy in their domain. Inviting them to demos and briefings is a form of cultural mourning — honoring the world that's ending by pretending it still applies.

The meaning crisis dimension is personal and existential: these people built their identity around being the ones who *understood* and *translated* technology. The technology is now outpacing their ability to understand it, and the audience they translated for may not be the audience that shapes what comes next.

**Potential angles for the post:**
- The structural economics of AGI vs. the niche tech market (the leverage math)
- The grief cycle: denial (AI is just a tool), anger (AI is dangerous!), bargaining (we need AI ethics), depression (I don't know what's happening)
- The labs' motivational structure: they're not cynically exploiting these influencers — they genuinely don't know which relationships matter in the new world either
- Who *does* have leverage in the AGI era? (Policymakers, compute owners, foundational researchers, and possibly people with direct societal scale access)

**Tone alert**: This idea risks sounding contemptuous. The interesting version is empathetic — these are people whose identity was built around something genuinely valuable (clear-eyed technical communication) who are now experiencing a structural displacement, not a personal failure. The meaning crisis is real and worth taking seriously.
